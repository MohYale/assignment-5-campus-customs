Ticket 101 — customer order

Tauhid Zaman wants 1 Classic Bulldog Tee, CC-TEE-WHITE, size S.

Expected

Inventory. The request is for one shirt in a specific size, so the first question is whether that size is on the shelf.

Delegations

  1. Boss calls inventory: check CC-TEE-WHITE, size S, quantity 1.
  2. Inventory finds the size is not in stock and names the vendor who can restock it. Inventory tells the boss. That vendor is Bulldog Print Co, apparel reprint, with 5 lead days.
  3. Boss calls accounting: is there enough cash to buy the shirt from that vendor?
  4. Accounting checks the checking account and tells the boss that there is enough. Checking holds $3,400.
  5. Boss calls customer service: draft an email to Tauhid Zaman. The shirt is not in stock. The shop will work to get it, expecting 5 days, and will email him with updates. The draft is not sent.

  • Boss: list_open_tickets, assess_ticket_request
  • Inventory: check_inventory_for_ticket or check_stock, then recommend_vendor
  • Accounting: read_cash, and check_margin so the unit cost can be set next to the cash balance
  • Customer service: draft_customer_message

Reflection

How the agents did

They did an excellent job. On each ticket they performed the task to my expectations: they read the request, used the shop tables, named the right next step, and left payment and any customer message for me to approve.

  • Ticket 101. They found that the size-S tee was not on the shelf, named Bulldog Print Co and the 5-day lead, and did not pay the $840 invoice. I approved the $8 restock myself.
  • Ticket 102. They treated it as rent, checked checking against $2,400, prepared the rent draft, and had facilities confirm the Chapel Street lease. I approved the payment.
  • Ticket 103. They found 8 hoodies on hand, a shortfall of 12, Bulldog Print Co, a $264 purchase at $22 each, and a customer draft that was not sent. I approved the purchase.

Expected plan compared with the executed plan

Ticket 101. I expected inventory first, then accounting to confirm cash for the shirt, then customer service to draft an email that would not be sent. The executed plan called inventory and accounting together. Inventory did the stock and vendor check I expected. Accounting reviewed invoice 501 and refused to treat it as proof the reprint had arrived, which is the careful version of “do not pay yet.” Customer service did not take a step, so the email draft was the one piece of my plan that did not run. The outcome still matched: out of stock, right vendor, no money moved until I approved the $8 purchase order.

Ticket 102. I expected accounting first, a draft for the $2,400 rent because checking could cover it, and then facilities. The executed plan called accounting and facilities in the opening step. Accounting prepared the rent draft, and facilities confirmed lease 1, $2,400, due 2026-09-02. They passed the lease id back and forth and prepared the draft twice, and the boss also looked at ticket 103 on the last step. The payment still waited for me, and checking fell from $3,392 to $992 only after I approved it.

Ticket 103. I expected inventory first, then accounting on the $264 restock, then a customer-service draft. The executed plan called accounting first and inventory in the same step. Inventory still reported 8 on hand and the shortfall of 12, accounting drafted the $264 purchase order, and customer service drafted the note to Yale AI Club and did not send it. No discount amount was on the ticket, so they reported the $58 list price, the $22 cost, and the $36 margin and left the price alone. I approved the purchase order, and checking went from $992 to $728.

One agent with tools

One agent with these tools would have been simpler. Each ticket is a short lookup: stock or lease, cash, a draft, and stop. The tasks do not have the degree of complexity that calls for a multi-level team. On 102 the lease amount was already on the ticket, and the agents still handed that fact around. On 103 the same purchase order was drafted twice. One agent could have called the tools in one pass.

New problems this team could handle

  • Similar tickets for different Campus Customs inventory. A hat, a mug, or another size is the same path: assess the ticket, check the shelf, name the vendor, and draft a purchase if the count is short.
  • Updating the checking account and keeping it clean. The agents prepare the payment, and the human approval posts it, so the balance stays a simple record of what I actually approved.
  • A customer asking for a discount. The team can read the list price and the unit cost, say what the margin is, and draft a reply. I still decide the price.

What this multi-agent team cannot handle

  • Ticket 103 started with 8 hoodies and we ordered 12 more. That purchase does not change inventory. I would add a tool that counts the received units onto the shelf.
  • Customer service can only draft a message. I would add an email address and a phone number so the customer-service agent can improve the experience once I approve the contact.
  • If a discount is offered, nothing writes the new price. I would add a tool that adjusts the price of the item to the discounted price.
